# Voice Audio Quality Assessment

**Assessment of an original, unprocessed spoken-word recording** from *Blood Hunt*, Chapter 1 excerpt.

## Project overview

This project assesses the technical and perceptual quality of the original dialogue recording. It considers recording quality, vocal characteristics, intelligibility, dynamics, tonal balance, and sibilance to identify the issues that matter and set priorities for any later work. The aim is to preserve the natural character and expression of the performance.

## Original recording

**File:** [Untouched original recording](001_Blood%20Hunt_CH1_Excerpt_Flat.mp3)

This assessment concerns the untouched original recording only. No processing has been applied to the audio presented for this assessment. Processing and the finished master are documented separately in **Voice Processing & Final Delivery**.

## Recording quality

The recording is clean and of good overall quality, with no noticeable background noise, clicks, or distracting lip noise. A small amount of lower-mid room tone is audible, but it is not a significant problem. Breaths are audible and sound natural, so they should be preserved rather than removed indiscriminately. The source does not appear to require extensive restoration.

## Dynamics and performance

The performance begins strongly, with natural level variations that contribute to the storytelling. These variations should not be removed simply to make the narration uniform. Any dynamic control should preserve expression while supporting intelligibility and appropriate consistency.

The wider chapter becomes quieter toward the end. Consistency should therefore be considered across the full chapter rather than judged from this short excerpt alone.

## Tonal balance and intelligibility

The recording has a slight excess of low-frequency energy and could benefit from a little more high-frequency presence. Any tonal refinement should reduce excess low end and improve clarity without making the voice unnaturally bright. The midrange should be preserved because it supports intelligibility and the natural character of the voice.

## Sibilance

More high-frequency presence can make sibilants more prominent. If that becomes harsh or distracting, de-essing may be appropriate. The goal is to control uncomfortable sibilance while retaining a natural voice, not to eliminate it.

## Assessment priorities

1. Address the slight excess of low-frequency energy while maintaining vocal clarity.
2. Preserve the storytelling and natural dynamics while managing overall level variation.
3. Consider consistency across the full chapter, especially as the narration becomes quieter toward the end.
4. Manage distracting sibilance if tonal refinement makes it more prominent.
5. Preserve natural breaths, expression, and the character of the performance.
6. Assess final audio against the applicable delivery requirements; exact numerical targets are not specified here.

## Summary

This clean, good-quality recording is a strong starting point for professional delivery. The main challenges are tonal refinement, dynamic management, sibilance control, and consistency across a longer recording—not extensive corrective restoration. The guiding principle is to improve clarity and technical consistency without compromising the natural performance.
